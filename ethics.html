Why is the study being done?

This study examines how different AI explanation styles relate to decisions, trust, confidence, and perceived workload in fictional monitoring situations. It is intended for academic research.

What will I do?

If you take part, you will complete three practice items and 30 fictional monitoring scenarios. You will see three explanation styles in randomized blocks, choose an action for each scenario, and answer short rating questions. At the end you will rate workload and may provide optional comments. The study takes about 15 minutes. No live AI system is used. Every recommendation is a fixed research stimulus and may be incorrect; your choices are not used for real-world decisions.

Who can take part?

Adults aged 18 years or older who can read the study language and provide informed consent. Do not participate if you do not meet the age requirement.

Is participation voluntary?

Yes. You can stop before final submission by closing the page. If you submit an incomplete or completed record to the study spreadsheet, contact the researcher and provide your participant code or session ID if available to request deletion. After data have been irreversibly anonymised or included in aggregate outputs, withdrawal may no longer be possible; the researcher must specify the applicable cut-off here: [Withdrawal cut-off and procedure].

What information is collected?

The study records a generated session ID, a participant code if you enter one (otherwise a random code), age group, country of residence, optional gender, education, AI and engineering/industry experience, scenario decisions, condition shown, response times, ratings, workload scores, optional free-text comments, consent timestamp, and completion timestamp. Do not include names or other identifying details in free-text answers.

Where is the information stored?

Responses are first kept in the browser's local storage. If the researcher enables Google Sheets collection in the published configuration, a copy is sent over HTTPS to a Google Apps Script endpoint and stored in a private Google Sheet controlled by the research team. The web app itself does not request or store IP addresses, use tracking cookies, or send data to analytics services. As with any website request, hosting and network providers may process technical connection data under their own terms. The researchers must restrict Sheet access to authorised staff; the public Apps Script endpoint can be called by anyone who has its URL, so this prototype should not collect special-category or highly sensitive data.
